1. Details
- The module computes double-precision floating-point multiplication by forming the significand product, adjusting exponent and sign, and normalizing the result path.
- `fpu_double` contains the instantiations of the other 6 modules, which are 6 separate source files for the 4 operations, the rounding module, and the exceptions module.
- As with the `fpu_add` module, the `fpu_sub` module passes the sign, exponent, and mantissa signals to the rounding module.
- If the FPU operation is addition and one operand is positive while the other is negative, `fpu_double` routes the operation to the subtraction module.

2. Sign and Operand Classification
2.1 Sign
- The result sign is `sign = opa[63] XOR opb[63]`.

2.2 Operand analysis
- On enable, extract `mantissa_a = opa[51:0]`, `mantissa_b = opb[51:0]`, `exponent_a = opa[62:52]`, and `exponent_b = opb[62:52]`.

2.3 Normal and zero detection
- Compute `a_is_norm = exponent_a != 0` and `b_is_norm = exponent_b != 0`.
- Compute `a_is_zero` when all bits `opa[62:0]` are zero.
- Compute `b_is_zero` when all bits `opb[62:0]` are zero.
- `in_zero = a_is_zero or b_is_zero`.

2.4 Mantissa preparation
- Construct 53-bit multiplicand values with hidden-bit insertion.
- `mul_a = {a_is_norm, mantissa_a}`.
- `mul_b = {b_is_norm, mantissa_b}`.
- For normalized operands, the hidden bit is 1. For denormals, the hidden bit is 0.

3. Partial-Product Decomposition
- The 53x53 mantissa multiply is decomposed into ten smaller unsigned multiplies.
- `product_a = mul_a[23:0] * mul_b[16:0]`.
- `product_b = mul_a[23:0] * mul_b[33:17]`.
- `product_c = mul_a[23:0] * mul_b[50:34]`.
- `product_d = mul_a[23:0] * mul_b[52:51]`.
- `product_e = mul_a[40:24] * mul_b[16:0]`.
- `product_f = mul_a[40:24] * mul_b[33:17]`.
- `product_g = mul_a[40:24] * mul_b[52:34]`.
- `product_h = mul_a[52:41] * mul_b[16:0]`.
- `product_i = mul_a[52:41] * mul_b[33:17]`.
- `product_j = mul_a[52:41] * mul_b[52:34]`.
- This decomposition is chosen so the multiplication can map efficiently into FPGA DSP resources instead of relying on a monolithic 53x53 multiply.

4. Partial-Product Accumulation
- The module does not sum all ten products in one expression.
- It forms staged sums and places each partial product at the appropriate bit offset.
- The functional requirement is that the final 106-bit product is mathematically identical to `product = mul_a * mul_b`.
- The implementation places the products with these effective shifts: `product_a` at offset 0, `product_b` at offset 17, `product_c` at offset 34, `product_d` at offset 51, `product_e` at offset 24, `product_f` at offset 41, `product_g` at offset 58, `product_h` at offset 41, `product_i` at offset 58, and `product_j` at offset 75.
- A recreated implementation may use a different adder tree as long as the final 106-bit product matches exactly.

5. Exponent Computation
5.1 Base exponent term
- Compute `exponent_terms = exponent_a + exponent_b`.

5.2 Bias removal
- The module subtracts 1022 rather than 1023 because the mantissa product may span `[1,4)` before final normalization.
- Compute `exponent_gt_expoffset = exponent_terms > 1022`.
- Compute `exponent_under = 1022 - exponent_terms` when `exponent_terms <= 1022`, else 0.
- Compute `exponent_1 = exponent_terms - 1022` when `exponent_terms > 1022`, else 0.

5.3 Left-normalization compensation
- The module counts the leading-zero distance in the 106-bit product using `product_shift`.
- Then compute `exponent_gt_prodshift = exponent_1 > product_shift_2`.
- `exponent_2 = exponent_1 - product_shift_2` when `exponent_gt_prodshift` is true, else 0.

5.4 Denormal underflow compensation
- If the exponent is too small, the product must be right-shifted to form a denormal result.
- The amount is derived from `exponent_under` and the normalization state.
- If the required right shift exceeds 52, the mantissa is shifted out and the result becomes zero. Later exception logic identifies this as underflow.

6. Product Normalization and Denormal Handling
6.1 Leading-zero count
- A combinational `casex` priority encoder computes `product_shift` from the 106-bit product.
- Only shifts up to 53 are needed. A larger count implies a case that necessarily underflows in this design space.

6.2 Normalize left when possible
- If the product does not already have its leading 1 at the expected top position, shift left by `product_shift_2` to create `product_1`.

6.3 Shift right for denormal underflow
- If `exponent_under` is nonzero, right-shift the product path by the required amount to form denormalized intermediate values such as `product_2` and `product_3`.

6.4 Zero handling
- If either input is zero, the mantissa and exponent path collapses to zero.

7. Output Mantissa Bundle
- The final mantissa bundle is `product_7 = {1'b0, product_6[105:52], product_lsb}`.
- Bit 55 is a reserved leading zero for possible overflow in the rounding stage.
- Bits 54:1 contain the leading integer bit, 52 mantissa bits, and the first rounding bit.
- Bit 0 is a sticky bit that is the OR of discarded low product bits.
- The low sticky information is captured by `product_lsb = OR` of the discarded remainder region after the final product alignment and denormal adjustment.

8. Output Exponent
- `exponent_5` is the final 12-bit exponent associated with `product_7`.
- `exponent_5` is zero for zero results and denormals after all underflow adjustments.
- `exponent_5` may exceed 2046 temporarily; the later exception module interprets this as overflow.

9. Required Sequential Behavior
- A functionally equivalent implementation must preserve the following staged behavior: register all extracted fields and classification signals, register the partial products, register the staged sums, register the 106-bit product, register the leading-zero count and exponent corrections, and register the final normalized or denormalized product bundle and exponent.
- The exact register naming need not match, but the cycle-by-cycle arithmetic meaning must match.

10. Functional Corner Cases
- Normalized x normalized produces hidden-bit 1 on both operands.
- Denormal x normalized or denormal x denormal uses hidden-bit 0 on the denormal input.
- Zero times anything produces a zero mantissa and exponent datapath.
- Very small products produce `exponent_5 = 0` and a right-shifted denormal mantissa bundle.
- Products with exponent above the finite range are not saturated here; they are passed onward for exception handling.
